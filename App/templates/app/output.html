Persistent Cough
Fever / Night Sweats
Dyspnoea on Exertion
Pleuritic Chest Pain
Unexplained Weight Loss
Haemoptysis (blood sputum)

Pneumonia, pulmonary TB, lung malignancy, interstitial lung disease, pulmonary oedema, haemorrhage, aspiration
Investigations
HRCT chest, sputum culture/AFB, bronchoscopy + BAL, PET-CT if malignancy suspected, spirometry

Bacterial pneumonia is an acute lower respiratory infection characterised by bacterial invasion of the alveoli, producing intense inflammatory exudate that impairs gas exchange. It causes classic lobar or segmental consolidation on imaging, often with air bronchograms.
Key Symptoms
High Fever (>39°C) + Rigors
Productive / Rusty Sputum
Tachypnoea / Breathlessness
Pleuritic Chest Pain
Severe Malaise
Bronchial Breath Sounds
Clinical Details
Common Pathogens
S. pneumoniae (50%), H. influenzae, S. aureus (MRSA risk), Klebsiella, Legionella (atypical)
First-Line Tx
Amoxicillin 500mg TDS or co-amoxiclav; add macrolide if atypical suspected
Severe Cases
IV co-amoxiclav + clarithromycin, O₂ therapy, IV fluids, ICU if sepsis/ARDS
Complications
Parapneumonic effusion, empyema, lung abscess, bacteraemia, sepsis, ARDS
Recovery Time
Symptoms improve 3–5 days; 1–3 weeks full recovery; X-ray clears 4–6 weeks
Imaging
Lobar/segmental consolidation, air bronchograms, parapneumonic effusion
